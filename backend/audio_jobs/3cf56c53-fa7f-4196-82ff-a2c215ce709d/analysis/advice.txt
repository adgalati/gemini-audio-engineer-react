This is a high-energy Pop-Rock track with a classic "quiet-verse, loud-chorus" structure. The arrangement is solid, but the mix currently suffers from some frequency masking and a lack of low-end definition, particularly when the full band enters.

As your Audio Engineer, here is a technical breakdown and a roadmap for your next mix revision:

### 1. Frequency Balance & Spectral Analysis
Looking at the spectrogram, there is a massive shift in energy at the **0:42 mark** (the chorus). While you want the chorus to feel "big," the spectrogram shows a very dense, almost "brickwalled" block of energy from 2kHz to 10kHz.

*   **The "Fizzy" High-Mids:** The distorted guitars in the chorus are competing heavily with the cymbals and the vocal's sibilance. I recommend a **wide, gentle bell cut of 2-3dB around 4kHz** on the guitar bus. This will clear out "fizz" and allow the lead vocal to cut through without needing more volume.
*   **Low-Mid Mud:** Between **200Hz and 500Hz**, there is a buildup during the chorus that makes the mix feel "congested" rather than "warm." Check your acoustic guitar tracks; they likely need a high-pass filter (HPF) pushed up to about 120Hz and a notch at 350Hz to stop them from clouding the bass guitar.

### 2. The Kick and Bass Relationship
The low end (below 100Hz) looks a bit inconsistent in the spectrogram.
*   **Kick Drum:** The kick has a good "click" (around 3-5kHz), but it lacks "thump." I’d suggest a narrow boost at **60Hz**.
*   **Sidechain Compression:** To help the kick punch through the dense chorus, apply a fast-acting sidechain compressor to the bass guitar, triggered by the kick. A gain reduction of just **2-3dB** whenever the kick hits will create the necessary "pocket" for the low end to feel tight.

### 3. Vocal Processing & Presence
The vocals are well-performed, but they sit "on top" of the mix rather than "inside" it.
*   **Body:** The lead vocal feels a bit thin in the verses. Try a small boost at **200Hz** to give the singer more "chest" resonance.
*   **Parallel Compression:** In the chorus (0:42+), the vocals are fighting the "wall of sound." Use a parallel compression bus (often called "New York Compression") with a very aggressive ratio (10:1) and blend it in. This will bring up the low-level details of the vocal performance without destroying the peaks.
*   **De-Essing:** There is some harshness on "S" sounds during the "Hold on to me" lines. Apply a de-esser centered around **7kHz** to smooth those transients.

### 4. Dynamics and Imaging
*   **Stereo Width:** The intro (0:00-0:14) is appropriately narrow. However, in the chorus, the guitars feel very "centered." To get that "pro" sound, hard-pan your rhythm electric guitars **100% Left and 100% Right**. This will clear the center of the mix for the kick, snare, bass, and lead vocal.
*   **The "Impact" Transition:** At 0:41, right before the chorus hits, try a **0.5dB gain automation drop** on the master fader for the last beat of the pre-chorus, then snap it back to 0dB (or +0.5dB) right as the chorus starts. This "breathing" technique makes the chorus feel like it's exploding outward.

### Summary of Recommendations:
1.  **Guitars:** HPF at 100Hz; cut 4kHz by 3dB to remove "fizz."
2.  **Bass:** Sidechain to the kick; cut 350Hz to remove "boxiness."
3.  **Vocals:** Parallel compression for the chorus; de-ess at 7kHz.
4.  **Mastering Prep:** Ensure you have at least **-3dB to -6dB of headroom** on your master fader before sending this for final mastering. The current chorus is hitting the ceiling quite hard, which limits what a mastering engineer can do with the transients.

The energy is definitely there—tightening the frequency "pockets" will make this track sound massive.
